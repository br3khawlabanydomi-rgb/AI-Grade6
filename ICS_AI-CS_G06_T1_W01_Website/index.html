Unit Launch - Rule-Based vs Learning Systems

Grade 6 · Main Course · Term 1 · Week 1 · Core + Lab, two 45-minute periods

Safeguarding and responsible use

Do not enter names, faces, voices, Emirates ID numbers, or other personal or sensitive information into any tool. Follow your teacher’s instructions. Use the printed or teacher-operated option; no student account is needed.

Starter

Activate: Show two short board clips or cards: a vending machine giving change (fixed steps) and a photo app grouping pictures of cats. Ask: which one was told exactly what to do, and which one seems to have figured something out? Teach: Introduce the big idea of the whole unit - some systems follow rules written by people, and some systems learn patterns from data. Give the plain definitions and one example of each. Apply: In pairs, students place three system cards (traffic light timer, spam-email filter, spell-checker suggestions) under 'fixed rules' or 'learns from data' and write one reason. Check: Each pair reads out one sorted card and its reason; you note which distinctions are secure and which are shaky for the KWL. Learning objectives and success criteria

  • Generate at least two questions about how rule-based and learning systems differ.
  • Classify three systems as 'follows fixed rules' or 'learns from data', justifying each.
  • Co-construct the unit success criteria and the responsible-use norms for AI tools.

I can…

  • I can ask at least two genuine questions about rule-based and learning systems.
  • I can sort three systems into 'follows fixed rules' or 'learns from data' and give a reason for each.
  • I can help write our class responsible-use rules for AI tools.

Explanation and examples

A rule-based system does exactly what a person has written into it, step by step. If a situation is not covered by a rule, the system cannot handle it, because it has no rules to follow. A learning system is different - instead of being given every rule, it is shown many examples and it finds patterns in that data. We call the pattern it builds a model. The same learning system can behave differently if you train it on different examples, because what it learns depends on the data it sees. This unit is the start of Phase 3, where you begin to think like an AI solution builder, so you need to tell these two kinds of system apart. Neither kind is magic - both are built and checked by people. Understanding which kind you are looking at helps you predict when a system will work well and when it might fail.

Source activity

Hands-on activity guide Overview: An unplugged sorting and questioning launch: students compare a rule-card machine (fixed steps) with a set of learn-from-examples cards, then sort three real systems and post questions. No devices or accounts are used by students. Grouping / timing: Pairs for sorting, whole class for the question wall and charter. Tool: Printed system cards and the baseline KWL sheet (teacher board demo only for the two clips). - Account-free for students. Any AI clip is a curated, teacher -shown board demo using non-personal examples only. Facilitation steps: 1. Watch the two short board clips and note which machine was told every step. 2. Read the two definitions and add one example of each to your page. 3. Sort the three system cards into 'fixed rules' or 'learns from data' with your partner. 4. Write one reason under each card - what is your evidence? 5. Post at least two genuine questions on the class question wall. 6. Agree one responsible-use rule and add it to the class charter. Unplugged fallback: If no board clips are available, read the two printed scenario cards aloud and sort using only the printed cards - the lesson runs fully unplugged. Formative assessment (aligned to the objectives) Formative check. • Targets: Baseline for the rule-based vs learning-system distinction and safe-use norms • Check: Diagnostic KWL + question wall + rule/learning sort (not graded) • Success criteria: Poses at least 2 genuine questions and sorts at least 2 of 3 systems with a reason. • Evidence: Sorted systems + question wall on the baseline sheet. • Feedback / adjustment: Cluster misconceptions to target Weeks 2-5 and plan the Primer; agree the responsible-use charter. Differentiation Representation: Pair each definition with an icon and a real photo-free example; colour-code the two sort columns. Action & expression: Allow sorting by placing physical cards, pointing, or speaking the answer to a partner. Engagement: Use familiar UAE examples (Salik toll gates as rule-based, a photo app grouping desert and beach pictures as learning). People of Determination: Provide pre-cut large-print cards and a two-symbol choice board (gear = rules, seed = learns) for students who find writing hard; allow a scribe. EAL: Give a bilingual glossary card for rule, learn, data, model, example; sentence frame 'This is ___ because ___'. Stretch / next tier: Ask students to invent a fourth system card and challenge another pair to classify and justify it.
